THE 3-SECOND TESTTilt under a directional lamp and rotate once. A line your fingernail catches is a scratch; one it glides over is usually a surface mark/hairline. If it repeats, skips, or is loud, say so plainly.

MEDIA · VISUAL TERMS

What your eyes are looking for

Hairline
Fine, light superficial line; normally does not catch a nail. Example: “A few light hairlines visible only under angled light.”
Scuff
Broad, cloudy rub mark from sleeve/storage contact; often cosmetic but can add noise. Example: “Light sleeve scuffs; no feelable scratches.”
Scratch
Distinct groove mark, especially if it catches a fingernail. Do not soften the word. Example: “Feelable scratch across A2; causes several light ticks.”
Groove wear
Dull, greyed, grainy groove area from repeated play/mistracking; usually audible as distortion or persistent noise.
Warp
Dish, edge, or wave deformation. State whether it affects play: “Mild edge warp; tracks normally.”
Spindle marks
Lines radiating from label hole; count heavy marks in your description.
Non-fill / pressing flaw
Manufacturing defect, often a zipper-like sound. Do not call it ordinary surface noise if you know it is pressing-related.
Residue / contamination
Fingerprints, sticker residue, dust, smoke film, or unknown spots. Clean safely; disclose what remains.

SLEEVE · WHAT TO DISCLOSE

Cover condition is its own grade.

Ring wear
Visible circular wear or impression from the record inside.
Seam split
State side and length: “2-inch top-seam split.”
Corner wear / bumps
Blunting, whitening, crushing, or fraying of corners. Photograph severe damage.
Crease / bend
Linear fold in cover; note whether artwork is broken or it is a light storage crease.
Edge / spine wear
Rubbing, chipping, whitening, fray, split, or unreadable spine.
Cut-out / punch hole / saw cut
Promotional or clearance mark. Always disclose.
Writing / sticker / residue
Name location and whether removable: “price-sticker residue front cover.”
